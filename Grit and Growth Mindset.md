## Grit

Having grit means understanding that doing well in school and life relies on learning new things quickly rather than relying on fixed intelligence or talent. Grit requires stamina and sticking with your future over time like a marathon rather than a sprint. It works from the understanding that failure is not permanent, the ability to learn is not fixed, and talent is unrelated to grit.

## Growth Mindset

A growth mindset is the belief that skills can be built and that anyone can learn, leading people to focus on the process of getting better rather than just worrying about how they look or avoiding bad outcomes. Taking action with a growth mindset requires using four key ingredients: recognizing effort as a useful part of learning, taking on challenges, viewing mistakes as learning opportunities, and appreciating feedback instead of getting defensive.

## Internal Locus of Control

An internal locus of control is the degree to which you believe you have control over your life, which is essential for staying motivated and taking responsibility for what happens to you. You build this control by focusing on factors you can completely manage—such as your hard work and extra effort—rather than relying on uncontrollable factors like innate intelligence.

## How to Build a Great Mindset

Building a great mindset requires believing in your ability to figure things out, questioning self-limiting assumptions, and not letting your current knowledge box in your vision for the future. You take action by realizing everyone can grow and change, and by architecting your own life curriculum of books and audio materials around your passions and dreams instead of waiting for someone else or college to give you one.
