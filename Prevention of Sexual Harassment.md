# Understanding and Addressing Sexual Harassment

**Sexual harassment** includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal, non-verbal, or physical conduct of a sexual nature that creates an unfriendly, hostile, or offensive environment.

---

## Categories of Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment generally falls into two main categories:

### 1. Quid Pro Quo ("This for That")
Demanding sexual favors in exchange for career or academic benefits, promotions, raises, or job security.

### 2. Hostile Work Environment
Severe or pervasive conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, or uncomfortable atmosphere.

#### Examples of Verbal & Non-Verbal Behaviors:
* **Unwelcome Remarks:** Sexual comments, jokes, or stories.
* **Persistent Advances:** Repeatedly asking someone out after they have explicitly declined.
* **Inappropriate Comments:** Remarks regarding someone's body, clothing, or physical appearance.
* **Intrusive Inquiries:** Asking explicit questions about a person's private or sex life.
* **Gossip:** Spreading rumors or gossip of a sexual nature.
* **Explicit Displays:** Displaying sexually explicit posters, images, screensavers, or videos in shared spaces.
* **Digital Harassment:** Sending unwanted sexually suggestive emails, messages, emojis, or media.

---

## What to Do: Key Steps to Take

If you experience or witness sexual harassment, follow these steps:

### 1. Address the Conduct (If Safe)
If you feel safe doing so, clearly state to the individual that their behavior is unwelcome, inappropriate, and must stop immediately. Establishing a firm, explicit boundary can sometimes stop the behavior early.

### 2. Document Everything
Keep a detailed, private log of every incident. Make sure to record:
* Dates, exact times, and locations.
* Exactly what was said or done.
* Names of any witnesses present.
* Copies or screenshots of relevant text messages, emails, or chat logs.

### 3. Report Internally
File a formal complaint using established organizational channels:
* **Workplace:** Contact HR, an Internal Complaints Committee (such as the ICC / POSH Committee in India), or a direct supervisor.
* **Educational Institutions:** Contact the Internal Committee, Title IX coordinator, or campus safety.

### 4. Seek External Support & Helplines
Contact support services, counseling, or legal assistance if needed:
* **Emergency / Police:** Local emergency services (e.g., **112** / **100** in India).
* **National Helplines:** Women's Helplines (e.g., **1091** in India).
